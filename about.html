The Jean White Project

I’m an independent musician and The Jean White Project is my own music project. Making music has also meant thinking about everything around it: how to introduce a release, create content that feels genuine, understand what connects with listeners and keep moving without a large team behind you.

Those responsibilities can pull in different directions. A release plan, an artist’s identity and their content should support one another, rather than become a list of separate jobs competing for the same time and energy.

That is the thinking behind PROXIMA. I’m developing a more connected approach to artist marketing and inviting independent artists to help shape the first version of that work.

PROXIMA is at an early stage. I’m not claiming an established roster or presenting hypothetical work as a real result. The example campaign on the main site is there to explain the approach, not to suggest a track record that doesn’t exist yet.

What this should stand for

Better connected.
Still artist-first.

01

The music comes first

Marketing should make the artist’s point of view easier to recognise, not force every project into the same template.

02

Strategy should be practical

A plan has to fit the artist’s goals, resources and time — not just look good on a calendar.

03

Results should be real

When campaigns are under way, any public stream or performance updates should come from verified data and be shared with the artist’s agreement. Until then, there is no results counter to dress up.
